# 2020 Legacy: Retained Requirements, Retired Components, and Replacements

This document establishes the official traceability record of the 2020 coffee traceability pilot design against the 2026 architecture update for the Aroma Pacífico project. It identifies which 2020 requirements remain in force, which assumptions and components are retired or replaced, and how these architectural shifts impact connectivity, traceability, drone operations, and public lookup services.

The sole authority for requirements in this project is [`docs/case/case-statement.pdf`](case/case-statement.pdf). All architectural choices and assumptions documented here align with challenge point 1 (Gobierno) and Epic 1 of the case statement. This document satisfies pending decision PD-004 in the master [decision log](decision-log.md) and maintains cross-references with [ADR-001: Primary and Second Cloud](adr/adr-001-primary-and-second-cloud.md) and the [ADR index](decision-log.md#architecture-decision-records).

## 1. Exclusion of 2020 Quotes, Prices, Devices, and Cloud Services

The case statement explicitly mandates that all 2020 commercial quotes, hardware prices, device catalogs, and cloud service rates are excluded as valid quotations:

> "Precios, dispositivos y servicios de nube de 2020 no son cotización válida."
> — `docs/case/case-statement.pdf`, Contexto

Between 2020 and 2026, semiconductor supply chains, IoT hardware standards, carrier spectrum allocations, and cloud computing pricing structures underwent extensive transformations. Consequently:

- **Commercial quotes and hardware pricing**: All 2020 bill-of-materials costs, field sensor estimates, edge gateway rates, and contractor fees are formally retired. The 2026 architecture uses contemporary 2026 market pricing models structured across four distinct cost baselines: normal operating month, peak harvest month, single-farm hardware installation, and drone flight campaigns.
- **Physical device specifications**: Specific 2020 sensor hardware models and legacy modem references are retired. Field hardware for 2026 must satisfy the non-technical constraint of minimal fixed installation overhead, low capital expenditure for the coffee grower, autonomous power operation in the lot without reliable electrical mains, and resilience against high humidity and ambient temperature.
- **Cloud provider services**: 2020 cloud service references and architecture sketches are retired. The 2026 update establishes AWS as the primary cloud provider and prepares Microsoft Azure as the secondary cloud without duplicating platform services, as decided and justified in [ADR-001](adr/adr-001-primary-and-second-cloud.md).

## 3. Architectural Impact Analysis

The transition from the 2020 design to the 2026 architecture introduces four major system impacts across connectivity, traceability, drones, and public lookup.

### 3.1 Impact on Connectivity

The 2020 design relied on an optimistic connectivity assumption that proved unrealistic in rural Colombian topography: continuous cellular data across most farms and direct streaming from sensors into cloud endpoints. The 2026 update replaces this assumption with an offline-first, store-and-forward paradigm:

1. **Edge store-and-forward buffers**: Every farm monitoring device and transporter phone incorporates local circular buffering. When cellular signal is lost, telemetry is persisted locally for up to 24 hours. Once connectivity is re-established, the device uploads its backlog with rate-limiting and retry backoff, ensuring the cloud ingestion endpoint is not overwhelmed.
2. **Out-of-coverage farm integration**: Farms located in deep topographic dead zones operate without requiring the Federation to build costly telecommunications towers or schedule rigid weekly technician visits. Data is retrieved through asynchronous, low-overhead methods such as local storage offload when farmers or extension agents visit municipal centers.
3. **Structured collection center LAN and WAN**: Unlike the 2020 design, which left collection centers unmanaged, the 2026 architecture introduces a dedicated, segmented local area network and encrypted cloud WAN connection for each of the three departmental facilities. The IP plan strictly reserves non-overlapping subnets (`10.41.0.0/16` for Cauca, `10.42.0.0/16` for Huila, and `10.43.0.0/16` for Tolima) with edge firewalls enforcing permanent and temporary traffic inspection rules.
4. **Port boundary without dedicated infrastructure**: At Buenaventura, the platform avoids capital expenditure on physical networking hardware, leveraging carrier-grade cellular and web connections solely to register the bag handover event.
5. **Modern cellular radio access**: The platform excludes reliance on deprecated 2G or 3G technologies. Field hardware and IoT modems are restricted to active 4G LTE, LTE-M, or NB-IoT networks that operators maintain throughout the pilot lifecycle.

### 3.2 Impact on Traceability

Traceability in the 2026 platform maintains the integrity of the coffee chain of custody across every physical transformation, standardizing on the 60 kg coffee sack:

1. **Chronological integrity despite disconnections**: Because edge devices implement store-and-forward buffering with synchronized internal clocks, sensor telemetry packets carry original edge timestamps and sequence numbers. When buffered batches upload after an outage, the cloud processing pipeline reconstructs the time series accurately, preserving drying curves and fermentation alert sequences without data loss.
2. **Unitization in standard 60 kg sacks**: The 2020 requirement to track coffee in standard 60 kg bags remains the central traceability unit. From initial hulling and bagging at the farm or wet mill to palletization at the collection center and container loading at Buenaventura, the unique bag identifier anchors all environmental and logistical telemetry.
3. **Transporter journey continuity**: Transporters traverse mountainous terrain with frequent cellular blackouts. The transporter mobile application buffers location waypoints and time-stamped status events locally. When connectivity returns, the application synchronizes the complete route trail to the platform, ensuring no unexplained gaps exist between farm pickup and collection center drop-off.
4. **Clean boundary at maritime export**: Traceability under the Federation platform explicitly concludes upon registration of the bag handover at the Port of Buenaventura. Telemetry generated during international oceanic transit remains the responsibility of the shipping carrier, establishing an unambiguous legal and administrative demarcation.

### 3.3 Impact on Drone Operations

Drone services transition from an informal flight concept in 2020 to a tightly governed, multi-service agricultural capability in 2026, as formalized in [ADR-002](adr/adr-002-drone-flight-mode-and-governance.md):

1. **Combined flight mode**: The platform adopts a combined operational model: area agriculture for perimeter lot delimitation and harvest estimation, combined with precision agriculture for sensor alert confirmation, targeted spot fumigation, and post-application verification.
2. **Pre-positioned missions and offline execution**: Drones operate in remote parcels far from reliable cellular links and independently of farmer mobile phones. Flight missions (including boundary polygons and prohibited no-fly zones around houses, water bodies, roads, and wet mills) are pre-positioned onto the ground control station at municipal hubs or collection centers prior to takeoff.
3. **Mandatory log upload and closed-loop gating**: If a ground station loses connectivity during flight, mission telemetry and imagery remain in the drone and ground station buffer. The platform strictly enforces governance gates: a fumigation order cannot be closed or certified until the encrypted post-flight log and verification photograph are successfully uploaded to cloud storage.
4. **Role separation and identity governance**: Farmers do not fly drones or authorize chemical spraying. Drone operations require explicit role segregation: certified third-party commercial pilots execute flights, and licensed Federation agronomists authorize fumigation orders through multi-factor authentication (MFA).

### 3.4 Impact on Public Lookup and Buyer Experience

The 2020 concept of public inquiry is refactored into a privacy-preserving, read-only query service:

1. **Asynchronous eventual consistency**: Because field devices operate under store-and-forward buffering, public lookup queries reflect the state up to the last successful upload rather than real-time telemetry. The web portal clearly communicates update timestamps, preventing user confusion during temporary rural field disconnections.
2. **Role-based information projection**: The public bag query service satisfies consumer demand for origin transparency while enforcing strict privacy protections. When a buyer or consumer scans a 60 kg bag code:
   - **Exposed data**: Coffee botanical variety, department and municipality of origin, altitude, harvest and processing dates, certified drying moisture metrics, and current custody status (e.g., in lot, in transit, at collection center, delivered at port).
   - **Withheld data**: Farmer national identification number (cédula), exact residential coordinates, full GPS telemetry breadcrumbs of transport vehicles, and detailed aerial drone imagery.
3. **Separation of concerns**: Internal collection center operators and Federation agronomists view operational and logistical metrics, while external buyers access a read-only, cached projection hosted on scalable cloud infrastructure.
